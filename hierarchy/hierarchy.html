Voice-Over Voice Actor


by Tara Platt and Yuri Lowenthal

So you want to be a voice actor? Close your eyes for a moment and listen.
All of those noises you hear – the murmurs and whispers, dripping taps and distant engine rumbles – are waves on the ocean of sound we swim through each day. Our emotions respond most viscerally to what we hear. Our lives are constantly awash with sound, though we rarely acknowledge it. Sound is everything. If you don’t believe me watch a movie minus the post-production sound design. You’ll get a new appreciation for sound engineers, sound designers, and musicians.

The visuals may draw us in but it’s the sound that keeps us in. I believe this is why we hang on every word uttered by that chap who voices over movie trailers, and why we are equally compelled to go and beat up a mime.
Professional voice actors understand the importance of conveying information using sound. They’re among the most underappreciated and they’re among the most talented.

Consider that a film or television actor can convey emotion or meaning with a raised eyebrow or a nuanced expression. Voice actors enjoy no such advantage. If it can’t be done with just the voice, then it can’t be doness.

Since Visual Communication is my major I was already very familiar with the basics of visual hierarchy so it was honestly pretty fun to just cause chaos and just do whatever I want. Pretty much just applying the required tags wherever I thought would be funny. The one I thing I did want to give some hierarchy was make the book’s authors the largest element on the website. Even if the goal was to disrupt the hierarchy I’m still fans of both their works. And in the end of the day I am satisfied with everything.
It’s the designer’s job to display information to viewers. Arguably the most important job of any project. If a viewer were to see my site they’d be confused on why certain elements have more emphasis and move on with their day. The book’s title is arguably the hardest part to see on the site, and would have trouble finding the necessary info they’re looking for.
